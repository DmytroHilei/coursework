# Inline Question 2

Despite their recent success in large-scale image recognition tasks, ViTs often lag behind traditional CNNs when trained on smaller datasets. What underlying factor contribute to this performance gap? What techniques can be used to improve the performance of ViTs on small datasets?

**Your Answer**: fill this in.


The main reason Vision Transformers (ViTs) underperform compared to CNNs on small datasets is the lack of inductive bias. CNNs are designed with strong prior assumptions about images, such as locality (nearby pixels are related), translation invariance, and hierarchical feature extraction. These biases allow CNNs to learn meaningful visual features efficiently even with limited data.

In contrast, ViTs treat images as sequences of patches and rely on self-attention to learn relationships between them. They do not inherently encode spatial structure or locality, so they must learn these properties directly from the data. This makes ViTs significantly more data-hungry and harder to train effectively on small datasets, often leading to underfitting or unstable optimization.

To improve the performance of ViTs on small datasets, several techniques can be used:

Data augmentation (e.g., random cropping, flipping, color jittering) to artificially increase dataset diversity.
Regularization techniques such as dropout, weight decay, and label smoothing to prevent overfitting.
Pretraining on large datasets followed by fine-tuning on the target dataset, which allows the model to learn general visual features beforehand.
Hybrid architectures (e.g., CNN + ViT) that introduce convolutional inductive biases into the model.
Smaller model configurations (fewer layers, lower embedding dimension) to reduce overfitting and improve optimization.
Knowledge distillation from a stronger model (often a CNN) to guide the ViT during training.

These approaches help compensate for the lack of inductive bias and improve generalization when training ViTs on limited data.
