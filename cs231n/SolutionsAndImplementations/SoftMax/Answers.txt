**Inline Question 2**

Although gradcheck is a reliable softmax loss, for the SVM loss, once in a while, a dimension in the gradcheck may not match exactly. What could such a discrepancy be caused by? Is it a reason for concern? What is a simple example in one dimension where an SVM loss gradient check could fail? How would changing the margin affect the frequency of this happening?

Note that SVM loss for a sample $(x_i, y_i)$ is defined as: $$L_i = \sum_{j\ne y_i}\max(0, s_j - s_{y_i} + \Delta)$$ where $j$ iterates over all classes except the correct class $y_i$ and $s_j$ denotes the classifier score for $j^{th}$ class. $\Delta$ is a scalar margin. For more information, refer to 'Multiclass Support Vector Machine loss' on [this](https://cs231n.github.io/linear-classify/) page.

*Hint: the SVM loss function is not strictly speaking differentiable.*


$\color{blue}{\textit Your Answer:}$ *fill this in.*  


The discrepancy loss may occur when we achieve the point, where the maximum function is not differentiable (0, s_j - s_{y_i} + \Delta \approx 0). And due to the finite difference's small error, we may get a value that is not exactly equal to the analytic solution. 
This is not a problem; it happens quite rarely and gives quite a small error.
